About the font

You're seeing real Roobert on this Mac. The trial you installed sits first in the stack. On the phone it falls back to Onest — a locally installed desktop font can't reach iOS, and a trial licence doesn't cover self-hosting it as a webfont. Real Roobert here, substitute there, until you licence it.

Your question: what does the middle button do?

In the reference it's a dot-grid, and the bar only has two other actions — because that app's whole navigation lives behind that button. Yours has four sections plus the AI. So there are two honest options:

  • The one I built. The grid opens a small panel with your four sections (Money / Bills / Should I buy / Vault). The bar stays three items wide, which is what makes the reference look calm — a four-tab bar plus an AI button can't. Cost: switching sections is two taps instead of one.
  • Keep your current four-tab dock and drop the reference bar. One tap to switch, but the bottom of the screen gets busy again and you lose the "Speak / Add" labels that make the reference read as a doing surface rather than a navigating one.

I'd pick the first, because of how you actually use this app: you open it to say a thing or check this month. Jumping to Vault is a weekly action, not a per-session one. Two taps is the right price for it.

The other changes you asked for

  • Top-left is now just this month's income plus the last six months as a sparkline, with September picked out — same as the reference's "Total Income" card.
  • Transaction icons are white circles now, with a line glyph inside instead of a coloured tile. It reads much closer to the reference, and it solves a real problem: you don't have merchant logos, and a coloured initial was pretending you did.
  • The bars are struck as thin vertical lines, not solid blocks — that's the detail I'd missed, and it's most of the texture in those screens. Same treatment on the sparkline and the analytics chart.
  • Analytics: income above the axis, expenses below in red, month labels on the axis itself, callouts on the selected month. Tapping the income card gets you there; the bar swaps its right-hand action to Back.

The hero is now Available balance

Settled: the hero is your vault total, in ink, with no breakdown under it — and it can be hidden. That is a real change of product, not just of colour. The old home screen opened with how badly this month is going; this one opens with how much you have. The shortfall still exists — it lives in Analytics now, one tap away on the income card, where income and expenses are shown against each other and red actually means something.

Two small things that came out of it. The asterisks keep the digit count (£**,***.**), so a hidden balance reads as hidden rather than as empty — and tabular-nums stops the number jumping width when you toggle. And the eye now swaps to a struck-through eye when hidden, so the control says which state you're in rather than which state you're going to.
